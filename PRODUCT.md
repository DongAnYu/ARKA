# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Students, independent learners, teachers, and researchers who keep study material in Markdown and want to turn it into active-recall practice. They use A.R.K.A while preparing or reviewing material and need the workflow to remain understandable even when local or hosted model infrastructure is unavailable. During Recall, the scheduler should coordinate the review queue without requiring users to switch between spaces manually.

## Product Purpose

A.R.K.A is a local-first desktop study tool that converts Markdown notes into reviewable multiple-choice questions and schedules them with spaced repetition. Success means users can move confidently from their own notes to useful questions, understand what data leaves their device, and recover quickly when generation cannot run.

## Brand Personality

Focused, precise, and trustworthy. The product should feel like capable native software: concise during routine work, calm and specific when something fails, and never decorative at the expense of clarity.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS styling, playful or humorous error messages, oversized rounded surfaces, ornamental motion, lifestyle imagery, dashboard decoration that obscures the next task, and interfaces that obscure provider or privacy behavior. Do not mimic Obsidian's branding even though Markdown vaults are a core input, and do not make the learner act as the scheduler.

## Design Principles

- Keep the study task primary; interface chrome should recede behind the current workflow.
- Show scheduled review workload before raw question volume.
- Let the scheduler coordinate the default global queue; recall spaces are a focused escape hatch.
- Make local-first and remote-provider behavior explicit so users understand where their notes are processed.
- Turn empty and error states into clear next steps rather than silent failures.
- Prefer familiar desktop controls and consistent terminology over novel interaction patterns.
- Preserve user control by supporting preview, pause, cancellation, and review before saving.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and keyboard operation. Use text and icons together for status, never color alone. Respect reduced-motion preferences, keep focus states visible, and write errors in plain language that does not assume technical knowledge.
