# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It takes place primarily in the [[chloroplast]], an organelle containing the green pigment chlorophyll.

The overall equation is:

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Photosynthesis has two main stages: the **light-dependent reactions** and the **light-independent reactions** (also called the [[Calvin cycle]]).

## Light-Dependent Reactions

These reactions occur in the thylakoid membrane. Chlorophyll absorbs light most strongly in the blue and red wavelengths, which is why plants appear green — green light is largely reflected, not absorbed.

Key steps:
1. Light strikes chlorophyll in Photosystem II, exciting electrons.
2. Water molecules are split (photolysis), releasing oxygen as a byproduct and replacing the lost electrons.
3. Excited electrons pass along an electron transport chain, pumping H⁺ ions into the thylakoid space.
4. The H⁺ gradient drives ATP synthase to produce ATP (chemiosmosis).
5. Photosystem I re-energizes electrons, which are used to convert NADP⁺ into NADPH.

The products of this stage — ATP and NADPH — are used to power the Calvin cycle. Oxygen released here comes from water, not from carbon dioxide, which is a common misconception.

## The Calvin Cycle

The Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma of the chloroplast and does not directly require light, though it depends on the ATP and NADPH produced by the light-dependent reactions, so it typically stops shortly after darkness falls.

Key steps:
1. **Carbon fixation** — CO₂ is attached to a 5-carbon molecule (RuBP) by the enzyme RuBisCO, forming an unstable 6-carbon compound that splits into two 3-carbon molecules.
2. **Reduction** — ATP and NADPH from the light reactions convert these molecules into G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate).
3. **Regeneration** — Most G3P is recycled to regenerate RuBP so the cycle can continue; only a fraction is exported to build glucose and other carbohydrates.

## Limiting Factors

The rate of photosynthesis is constrained by whichever factor is most scarce at a given moment (Blackman's law of limiting factors):

| Factor | Effect when scarce |
|---|---|
| Light intensity | Slows the light-dependent reactions directly |
| CO₂ concentration | Slows carbon fixation in the Calvin cycle |
| Temperature | Affects enzyme activity, especially RuBisCO |

Increasing light intensity beyond a certain point no longer increases the rate of photosynthesis if CO₂ or temperature is the limiting factor instead — a frequent source of confusion, since students often assume more light always means more photosynthesis.

## Photosynthesis vs. Cellular Respiration

Photosynthesis and [[cellular respiration]] are often confused because they are near-opposites:

- Photosynthesis consumes CO₂ and water, and releases O₂; respiration does the reverse.
- Photosynthesis stores energy in glucose; respiration releases that energy as ATP.
- Photosynthesis occurs only in cells with chloroplasts; respiration occurs in nearly all living cells, including plant cells themselves.

Plants therefore perform both processes — they photosynthesize during daylight but respire continuously, day and night.
